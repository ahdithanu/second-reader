§ 4Takeaways

3×

Agency buys triage, not automation. When the agent says "a human should see this," it's right 39% of the time — three times the hit rate of a confidence threshold saying the same thing (12%). But its review queue is 4.5× bigger.

$4.88

Per extra catch point, per 1,000 items. The agent costs 3.25× the single call and buys +10.6 points of catch. The tokens are cheap; the queue of humans it summons is the real bill.

−4.5pts

The verifier is a published negative result. A second agent attacking every verdict raised precision to 89.8% — and lost catch, doubled review load, and cost 62% more. Dominated at all 63 swept configs.

44%

Position bias stayed hard for every grader. Even holding the annotator's all-A voting history, no per-item grader convicts a defensible-looking single vote on pattern evidence. 10 of 18 sailed through everything…

$0 → 100%

…so the hill-climb closed it without a model. Layer 0: two deterministic per-annotator signals — side-vote ratio and justification reuse — flag workers, not items. All 4 planted defective annotators caught, zero false positives. Position bias 22% → 100%; the $23/1k single call plus this free layer now beats the $75 agent on every axis.

§ 6What it provably does not catch

  • Position bias per-item — CLOSED by Layer 0. 10 of 18 biased-annotator items passed every per-item grader; annotator-level aggregation took it 22% → 100% for $0 (see the hill-climb table). Kept as the project's one completed loop: measured miss → named cause → shipped fix → number moved. aggregates
  • The agent under-flags what it could auto-reject. Five items the single call flagged outright were escalated instead — caught, but at human cost. report --mode single|agent --defect RUSHED
  • The verifier overturns correct rejects. "Do not rubber-stamp" produced overturns of true positives too. report --mode verifier
  • Budget-starved runs. 1.3% of agent runs hit the 6-call cap and verdicted on partial evidence — recorded, not retried. trace --item-id <id>
  • Defect rates below the pattern threshold. An annotator boilerplating 3 items in 10 defeats both the per-item graders and Layer 0's thresholds. Still the system's blind spot — named so it isn't mistaken for covered.
